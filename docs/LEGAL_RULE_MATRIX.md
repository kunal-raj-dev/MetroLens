## 1. Statutory Architecture & Decriminalization Reality (Jan Vishwas Act, 2023)

### Crucial Legal Correction on Section 36 Penalties
Previous project research and competing hackathon proposals falsely claim that software can "issue automated fines or compounding notices of ₹25,000 to ₹1,00,000". **Under current Indian law as of 2024–2026, this is legally false.**

1. **Decriminalization & Improvement Notices (Section 36(1)):**
   - The **Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023** fundamentally overhauled Section 36(1) of the Legal Metrology Act, 2009.
   - For pre-packaged commodities not conforming to declarations (Rule 6 violations, labeling defects, font sizes), the law now mandates an **Improvement Notice** for the **First Offence**.
   - The manufacturer/packer/importer is granted a statutory window (typically 15–30 days) to rectify the non-compliance. If rectified, the case is formally closed with **no penalty**.
   - Penalties apply only upon failure to comply with an Improvement Notice or for repeated offences (adjudicated by an Adjudicating Officer appointed under Section 48A, not via automatic software summons).
2. **Short-Weight / Under-Measure (Section 36(2)):**
   - For net quantity shortages (e.g., selling 80g in a package declared as 100g), penalties remain stringent (first offence penalty up to ₹1,00,000; second up to ₹5,00,000).
   - **Critical Physical Boundary:** A monocular camera **cannot weigh an object**. Software must explicitly declare that Section 36(2) physical weight verification requires a certified physical check-weighing scale under Rule 24.
3. **Evidentiary Role of the System (Section 15 & Section 65B / Section 63 BSA):**
   - Under **Section 15** of the Legal Metrology Act, 2009, a Legal Metrology Officer (LMO) has the power of inspection, entry, and seizure.
   - The software functions as a **Prima Facie Evidentiary Audit Tool**: it captures tamper-evident proof (SHA-256 hash, GPS coordinates, timestamp, metric optical measurements) that provides statutory justification for an officer to issue an Improvement Notice or seize physical samples under Form 1.

### Statutory Language Guidelines for Software Output
1. **Never Output:** "This package is 100% legally compliant under Indian Law."
2. **Always Output:** "Image-Verifiable Statutory Compliance Assessment: Compliant with visual declarations under Rules 6, 7, 8, 9, 11 of LM(PC) Rules, 2011."
3. **Never Output:** "Penalty of ₹25,000 imposed."
4. **Always Output:** "Potential Statutory Non-Compliance Flagged. Recommended Regulatory Action: Issue Improvement Notice under Section 36(1) (as amended by Jan Vishwas Act, 2023) or verify physical sample under Section 15."
